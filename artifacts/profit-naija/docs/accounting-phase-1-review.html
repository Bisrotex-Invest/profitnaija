1. Agreed framework and policy register

IFRS means International Financial Reporting Standards. IAS means International Accounting Standards: the older standards adopted into the IFRS framework, which continue to apply unless replaced. These are not two competing systems. Apply each relevant standard and its effective requirements together.

3. Target report specification under IFRS / IAS

  • Sales: Cash, Bank and Credit sales before returns, excluding VAT. Debtor collections are not Sales.
  • Revenue: recognised consideration for satisfied performance obligations, net of returns and relevant discounts, excluding third-party taxes. For ordinary delivered trading goods this is base Sales minus base customer returns. Deposits/unfulfilled orders do not automatically qualify as revenue.
  • Purchases: received inventory at incurred landed cost before/after supplier returns as explicitly labelled. This is an acquisition metric, not Cost of Sales.
  • Cost of Sales: sum of locked landed cost released by sales, net of linked saleable customer-return reversals, plus separately identified inventory-loss adjustments where appropriate. Reconcile to Opening Inventory + net inventory additions − Closing Inventory; disclose adjustments instead of hiding differences.
  • P&L: Revenue − Cost of Sales = Gross Profit; less operating expenses (including depreciation), then finance costs, then separately labelled applicable tax estimate/provision. Equipment acquisition and loan principal are not ordinary P&L expenses.
  • Position balances: Cash, Bank, Inventory, Debtors and Creditors include verified opening balances and all valid movements through the chosen end date. Add assets, tax balances, loans and equity before presenting a complete balance sheet. Inventory reflects lower of cost and net realisable value; receivables include applicable expected-credit-loss allowances.
  • Cash Flow: actual gross receipts/payments across Cash and Bank, split into operating, investing and financing. Exclude credit notes, accruals and depreciation. Internal transfers cancel. Opening total money + net movement = closing total money; also reconcile separately to independent account balances and bank evidence.
  • VAT: output VAT − eligible input VAT ± credit-note/other adjustments − remittances = outstanding VAT, subject to validated applicability. Existing output-only totals must not be represented as a complete statutory payable.
  • WHT: amount withheld − remittances ± corrections = outstanding liability, supported by supplier and remittance records.
  • Period rule: activity uses the inclusive selected dates; balances are as of period end. Use the same dates and accounting rules in cards, detail screens and exports on both apps.
  • Precision: calculations retain supported monetary precision; round displayed P&L lines to whole naira as previously requested. Disclose presentation-rounding differences.

Inventory method

Selected app policy for interchangeable trading goods: moving weighted average. After a receipt, new average landed unit cost = (prior cost basis + new eligible landed cost) ÷ (prior units + received units). A sale releases the appropriate carrying amount; maintain cost and any net-realisable-value allowance separately so write-downs are not confused with purchase-price changes. Cost snapshots, quantities, chronological ordering and source links must be immutable/auditable.

IAS 2 permits, but does not mandate, weighted average: FIFO is also permitted for interchangeable goods; specific identification applies to non-interchangeable items. Use the same formula for inventories with similar nature and use. The selected moving-average policy preserves the existing direction while replacing uncertain separate ancillary reconstruction with an auditable landed-cost basis. Do not use LIFO. Eligible costs exclude recoverable taxes, selling costs, unnecessary storage and abnormal waste; assess charges by substance rather than capitalising every “other cost”.

Example: receive 10 units at ₦100 plus ₦200 freight, then 10 at ₦200 plus ₦100 freight. Total cost is ₦3,300 for 20 units: ₦165 per unit. Sell 5: Cost of Sales ₦825 and Closing Inventory ₦2,475. Refunds use the original sale cost, not a later product price.

The current base-cost moving average plus reconstructed ancillary allocation is not an assured historical landed-cost subledger. FIFO would require reliable layer history not currently established; switching to FIFO would increase historical reconstruction needs.

Opening-balance treatment

Choose a cutover date per merchant after the implementation design. Verify Cash, each bank account, inventory quantity/cost, individual open customer/supplier balances, fixed assets, accumulated depreciation, taxes, loans and supported equity. Introduce balanced opening entries supported by documents and an audit trail. An unresolved difference must remain visibly pending investigation, not silently become Sales or assumed owner capital.

For later periods, opening balances equal prior-period closing balances. Missing openings are unknown/unverified, not economically zero. Continue displaying the legacy zero-based recorded-money view as such until supported openings exist. The approved “older payments were Cash” classification is retained but does not prove the historical cash balance.

Specification supersession: the owner's IFRS/IAS instruction replaces the earlier fixed-zero opening-stock convention in the target rules. The application remains unchanged in Phase 1. A documented cutover can establish supported new records, but it does not by itself resolve IAS 8 retrospective correction or IFRS 1 transition requirements for statutory statements.

Why fixed-zero opening stock must be a decision: opening inventory ₦1,000, purchases ₦0, closing inventory ₦800, and sales ₦500 imply Cost of Sales ₦200 and Gross Profit ₦300 (ignoring other adjustments). Current zero-opening treatment gives Cost of Sales −₦800 and Gross Profit ₦1,300. This is a ₦1,000 overstatement versus the carried-forward basis. This example is synthetic; no merchant records were changed.

6. Merchant / platform separation

  • Merchant financial reports draw only from that merchant's business books. A Paystack/RevenueCat subscription receipt belongs to ProfitNaija's platform books, not merchant Sales, Cash or Debtors.
  • A merchant may record the actual ProfitNaija subscription they paid as a business software expense, once, using payment evidence. Platform checkout/webhook events must not automatically become merchant trading revenue or duplicate that expense.
  • Partner commissions, target bonuses and payouts belong to their own partner/platform ledger. They must not be merged into merchant profit merely because an identity/email is shared. If a partner has a separate business that earns commission, it needs an explicit supported transaction in that separate business book.
  • Platform accounting needs its own subscription revenue, tax, processor fees, refunds, deferred revenue where applicable, partner commission liabilities and payouts. This review does not certify those books or modify billing.
  • Server-side merchant ownership, partner-role isolation and distinct source IDs must remain enforceable; reports must not cross scopes.

7. Records and controls required before corrections

A later read-only merchant audit must check missing quantities/cost snapshots, invalid dates/amounts, stock movements without accounting effects, settlement links to missing originals, duplicated source IDs, overpayments, return quantities above the source transaction, missing supplier payable components, product/ledger stock differences and unsupported openings. Report exceptions by affected record and date, with no automatic repair.

Future corrections must preserve originals, document reason/evidence, distinguish entry date from economic date, respect the existing restriction on editing older stock events, use compensating entries after subsequent stock activity, and prevent repeated migration effects. Proposed historical corrections require separate owner approval and a reversible plan.

Operational compensating entries must not hide a material prior-period financial-reporting error in current-period profit. Under IAS 8, assess materiality and retrospective restatement separately, including the impracticability exception and required disclosures. Changes in estimates from new information are treated prospectively, not mislabelled as errors.

Acceptance examples for later implementation

  1. Cash and Bank versions of the same sale yield identical Revenue/Cost of Sales but different money-account movements.
  2. A credit sale followed by two collections recognises sales/VAT once and reduces only the linked receivable.
  3. A credit purchase or expense followed by settlement recognises acquisition/expense once, then reduces its payable.
  4. Customer/supplier credit notes change the correct debt balance without fictitious cash movement; actual refunds change the selected money account.
  5. The weighted-average example and carried-opening-stock example in this document reconcile on mobile, web and exports.
  6. Equipment purchases and credit settlements remain investing; depreciation is non-cash; loan principal/owner flows are financing; Cash↔Bank transfers have zero profit/net total-money effect.
  7. Tax remittance reduces money and tax liability, not Revenue; unsupported statutory estimates remain labelled provisional.
  8. Every prior closing balance carries forward; each report's date range matches its details/exports; platform receipts never enter merchant totals.
